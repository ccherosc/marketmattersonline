Your Google Business Profile (GBP) is the most powerful free marketing tool a local business has. When someone searches for your service in your area, your GBP determines whether you appear in the Google Maps "Local Pack" — those three listings that appear at the top of local search results.

Getting into that three-pack can double or triple your inbound leads without spending a dollar on ads.

Here's exactly how to optimize it.

---

## Why GBP Matters More Than Your Website for Local Businesses

Most local searches don't lead to a website visit. They lead to a phone call, a directions request, or a message — directly from the Google Maps listing. 

If you're a plumber, a dentist, a landscaper, or any service business, your GBP is the first and often *only* thing most potential customers see before they decide to contact you.

A well-optimized GBP outranks websites that have been around for years. That's the opportunity.

---

## Step 1: Claim and Verify Your Profile

Go to business.google.com and search for your business. If it's unclaimed, claim it. Google will send you a postcard with a verification code to your business address.

**If you work from home or have no storefront:** Set up a service area business. You don't need a physical address visible to the public.

---

## Step 2: Choose the Right Categories

Your primary category is the most important selection you make. Google uses it as the primary signal for what searches to show you for.

**Rules:**
- Pick the *most specific* category that matches your core service
- Add secondary categories for adjacent services you offer
- Don't add categories for things you don't actively offer — it dilutes relevance

Examples:
- Roofing contractor (not just "Contractor")
- HVAC contractor (not just "Heating contractor")
- Family law attorney (not just "Attorney")

---

## Step 3: Write a Keyword-Rich Business Description

You get 750 characters. Use most of them. This is not a place to be vague.

Include:
- Your primary service
- Your service area (city or region)
- What makes you different
- Years in business or notable credentials
- A secondary keyword or two

**Example:**
"Dallas Plumbing Solutions provides emergency plumbing repair, water heater installation, and drain cleaning services to homeowners and businesses in Dallas, Plano, and Frisco. Licensed master plumbers with 15+ years of experience. Same-day service available 24/7. 4.9 stars across 200+ Google reviews."

---

## Step 4: Complete Every Section

Most businesses leave these blank. Don't:

- **Services:** List every specific service with a description. This helps GBP match you to more specific searches.
- **Products:** If applicable, list your products with photos and pricing.
- **Attributes:** Select all that apply (women-owned, veteran-owned, online appointments, etc.)
- **Business hours:** Keep these accurate. Inaccurate hours are one of the fastest ways to lose trust.
- **Phone number:** Use a local area code number — not a toll-free number.
- **Website:** Link to your homepage or a specific landing page.

---

## Step 5: Upload Photos (This Matters More Than You Think)

Businesses with photos get 42% more direction requests and 35% more website clicks than those without. Photos signal that the business is active and real.

**What to upload:**
- **Exterior photos** (at least 3) — so customers know what they're looking for
- **Interior photos** — builds trust before they arrive
- **Team photos** — people buy from people
- **Work photos** — before and afters, jobs in progress, completed projects
- **Equipment or vehicles** — adds legitimacy

Upload at least 20 photos when you start. Continue adding photos monthly.

**Video:** GBP also supports short videos (30 seconds, max 75MB). Even one video sets you apart from competitors.

---

## Step 6: Build and Manage Your Reviews

Reviews are both a ranking factor and a conversion factor. Google looks at quantity, quality, and recency.

**Getting reviews:**
1. Ask every satisfied customer — in person, by text, by email
2. Make it easy with a direct link (get your short GBP link from the dashboard)
3. Text is the most effective channel: "Hi [Name], we appreciate your business! Would you mind leaving us a Google review? It really helps. Here's the link: [link]"
4. Set up a system — don't rely on memory. Use your CRM or a simple spreadsheet

**Responding to reviews:**
- Respond to every review, positive and negative
- Positive responses: Thank them and mention a specific detail from their review
- Negative responses: Acknowledge their concern, apologize, and offer to resolve it offline
- Never argue with a negative review publicly

**Target:** 50+ reviews at 4.5+ stars for competitive markets. For less competitive areas, 20+ reviews often gets you into the top 3.

---

## Step 7: Post Weekly

GBP posts are like social media posts — but for your Google listing. Businesses that post regularly signal to Google that they're active and engaged.

Post ideas:
- Service tips relevant to the season
- Limited-time offers or promotions
- Announce a new service
- Highlight a recent customer success
- Answer a common question

**Format:** Use a strong headline, a clear image, and one call-to-action button. Posts expire after 7 days by default, so post at least weekly.

---

## Step 8: Use GBP Messaging and Q&A

**Messaging:** Enable the messaging feature so prospects can text you directly from your listing. Respond within an hour during business hours — Google shows your average response time to users.

**Q&A:** Anyone can ask a question on your GBP, and anyone can answer. Monitor this weekly. Add your own Q&As preemptively — write the question and answer it yourself. This is free content that helps searchers and improves your profile's relevance.

---

## The Signals That Drive Local Rankings

Google uses three primary factors for local rankings:

1. **Relevance** — How well your profile matches the search query (categories, description, services)
2. **Distance** — How close you are to the searcher (you can't control this, but setting service areas correctly matters)
3. **Prominence** — How well-known and trusted your business is (reviews, links, citations, GBP activity)

Focus on what you can control: complete your profile, get reviews, post consistently, and build citations on other directories.

## Quick-Start Checklist

- [ ] Claim and verify your GBP
- [ ] Select the right primary and secondary categories
- [ ] Write a 750-character business description with keywords
- [ ] Add complete services list with descriptions
- [ ] Upload 20+ photos
- [ ] Set accurate hours
- [ ] Enable messaging
- [ ] Send review requests to your last 10 customers
- [ ] Post your first GBP update this week
- [ ] Add 5 Q&As to your profile
